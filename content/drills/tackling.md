title: Tackling

![Tackling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cecLjyTUlTQ)

Helmet-free, rugby-style tackling progression (Bobby Vernon, Palmetto Panthers). Live reps without pads condition players to keep the head out of contact and stay disciplined in form. 8-drill progression, low to full speed.

## 1. Run, Buzz, and Breakdown (00:05:39)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=339"></iframe>

Approach drill working the chase to contact, plus stance/hit position.

- Defender starts in a leveraged (inside-shade) position on the ball carrier
- Ball carrier runs cone-to-cone at varying speed, slowing to simulate a cutback
- Defender "buzzes" his feet to hold leverage/shade as the carrier slows
- Track the carrier's near hip with the eyes — don't get pulled off by hands, head, ball, arms, or feet
- Square the shoulders more as the defender closes the distance
- Breakdown/hit position: head up, hips sunk, knees bent, slight arch in the back, hands ready
- Power step is angled toward the target (not straight ahead) to keep the shoulder leading and the head further from contact

## 2. Hip Rip and Wrap (00:09:18)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=558"></iframe>

Two-part drill moving from the contact point through the follow-through.

- Part 1 (no bag): players on knees in hit position, tops of feet flat on the ground so they use hips, not toes; explode up with hips and arms
- Part 2 (with bag): same movement but hit the bag with the front of the shoulder, rip the arms, wrap and squeeze the bag together
- Head stays up and to the side, never dropped or driven straight on
- Contrast with the "double uppercut" heads-up teaching: this progression avoids a straight, head-on finish

## 3. Live Tackle from the Knees ("Panther Tackle") (00:11:39)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=699"></iframe>

First live-player rep, no helmets/pads, starting from the knees to control speed and size mismatch risk.

- Tackler starts on knees in the breakdown position (arched back, head up)
- Standing partner is tackled by ripping the arms and wrapping with enough hip drive to bring him down
- Work both the left- and right-shoulder tackle
- Match players of similar size since both start from their knees
- These leveraged shoulder tackles (low, high, or with a roll) are called "Panther tackles" throughout the rest of the progression
- Do NOT teach "wrapping around" the carrier — that drops the head; instead wrap/squeeze with the head to the side, shoulder leading

## 4. Full-Speed Dummy Bag Targeting (00:13:44)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=824"></iframe>

Tempo increases against full-size tackling dummies to drill strict targeting.

- Strict shoulder placement only — never lead with the head
- Target area: thigh/hip for low and roll tackles; near pec/near number for high tackles
- Good hip explosion, arms rip/wrap/squeeze on contact
- Sufficient leg drive (or roll) to move or take down the dummy
- Players can start two-point or three-point depending on position
- Work both left- and right-shoulder tackles; colored tape on the bag reinforces the legal target zones
- Roll-tackle variant: roll instead of driving the legs, still targeting the near thigh/hip

## 5. Live Shoulder Tackle ("Panther Tackle") (00:16:38)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=998"></iframe>

First live, moving-ball-carrier tackle without helmets/pads.

- Two players ~6 yards apart run to a cone/spot ~5 yards away, where the defender makes the live tackle
- Work both left- and right-shoulder tackles; increase distance to add more approach/tracking work
- Speed guidance: smaller players (<100 lb) can go 3/4 speed; larger players (200+ lb) should stay at 1/4-1/2 speed
- Hip and thigh pads recommended (not required) to reduce minor-injury risk during live reps

## 6. Cutback Pursuit Tackle (00:19:01)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=1141"></iframe>

Adds distance and a cutback element to condition pursuit discipline.

- Increase the space the ball carrier can operate in, forcing the defender to hold leverage while pursuing
- Run this from the numbers to the sideline, simulating a carrier getting vertical then cutting back
- Flip the drill to work both left and right while staying on the same side of the field

## 7. High Shoulder Tackle with Shield (00:21:32)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=1292"></iframe>

Same live-tackle mechanics as the low Panther tackle, but targeting high and without taking the player to the ground.

- Ball carrier holds a protective shield/vest; defender does not bring him down
- Target area shifts to the near pec/near number instead of hip/thigh
- Two players ~6 yards apart, working at about half speed; increase distance for more approach/tracking work
- Cues: eyes on near number/near pec, maintain leverage, shoulder to the target, head on the near side, eyes up, arms rip and wrap, good leg drive
- If bringing players to the ground, use a landing pad at a designated contact point

## 8. Full-Speed Game Simulation Tackles (00:23:43)

<iframe class="external-embed youtube" allow="fullscreen" frameborder="0" width="600px" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cecLjyTUlTQ?start=1423"></iframe>

Final drill: full-speed Panther tackles (low, high, and roll) in game-like situations.

- Place full-size tackling dummies at contact points matched to realistic pursuit angles
- Vary distances by position: shorter for linebackers/defensive ends, longer for defensive backs
- Simulate outside runs and short pass concepts (slants, hitches, spots, speed-outs) — room to add more routes/angles
- Ball carrier sprints to just behind the dummy and clears out so the defender finishes a full-speed tackle
