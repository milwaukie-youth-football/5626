---
title: Defense Positions
---

5-3 base man. Front: DE - DT - NT - DT - DE. Second level: OLB - MLB - OLB. Back: CB - S - CB.

---

## NT (Nose Tackle)

**Stance** — 3-point or 4-point, kid's choice. Foot placement is not a coaching point.

**Alignment** — Head up on the center.

**Strength call** — The **MLB calls the strength.** Rule at this level: whichever side has more kids. That's enough.

**First step** — Step toward the gap. Dip and rip, stay skinny if he can do it. *(Stretch goal, not a requirement.)*

**Run responsibility** — Slant to the **A gap on the strength side.** Every play.

**Doubled by center and guard** — Stay low and split the gap. Don't get moved.

**Pass** — Keep going. Get to the QB however he can.

**The cue** — *"Stay low and physical. Mess up their play."*

> Pairs with MLB: NT takes the strength A gap, MLB has the backside A gap. Between them both A gaps are covered every snap.

---

## DT (Defensive Tackle) — two of them

**Stance** — 3-point or 4-point, kid's choice.

**Alignment** — Head up on the offensive tackle. Always.

**First step** — To the gap.

**Run responsibility** — Slant to the **B gap. Every play, run or pass.**

**Pass** — Keep going. Get to the QB however he can.

**The cue** — *"Stay low and physical. Mess up their play."*

> **Fallback:** if they can't slant to B effectively, move them to start in the B gap instead.
>
> **Later teach, not week 1:** what to do when the offensive tackle blocks down on him. Trap, duo and pin-and-pull all do this. We'll get there if we get there.

---

## DE (Defensive End) — two of them

**These are boxing DEs.** Think Pittsburgh's 3-4 outside linebackers. The entire point of this defense is that our DEs stop every wide run in both directions. Sweeps, jets, all of it.

**Stance** — Sprinter's stance. Outside leg back, inside leg up.

**Alignment** — Close enough to draw the attention of the OT or TE. **Not so close that he can get folded and the edge is destroyed.**

> We do not want to depend on our corners to set the edge. They are our worst tacklers and run stoppers.

**Get off** — Quick. Stay low. Inside arm into the chest of the man in front of him.

**Get to your spot** — Upfield about 4 yards, same depth as a QB in pistol or shotgun.

**Run responsibility** — **Contain.** Turn everything back to the middle of the field.

**Never allowed inside.** Ever.

**Pass** — Once he's at his spot, rush the QB from there.

**The cue** — *"DON'T BREAK CONTAIN."* Over and over, all season.

---

## MLB (Mike) — captain on the field

**This is our best tackler.** Everything below is built to keep him in the middle of the field.

**Stance** — Athletic stance, wrists resting on top of the knees.

**Alignment** — Stacked behind the NT. **4-5 yards off the ball.**

**Read** — His man out of the backfield. RB or QB.

**First steps** — **Two read steps at the snap. Every LB, every play.** Then read and react.

**Run responsibility** — **Backside A gap.**

**Man coverage** — His guy is always someone in the backfield, which is how we keep him in the middle.

**Blitz** — On my call from the sideline. Signal: **both hands, pistols in the air.** He blitzes through the backside A gap. NT still takes the strength A gap — nobody's assignment changes on the blitz call, Mike just attacks instead of reading.

**Captain duties** — Calls the strength of the offense **every play**: *"strong left," "strong right."* Rule at this level: whichever side has more kids.

**The cue** — Mostly about helping everyone else line up.

> Pairs with NT: NT slants to the strength A gap, Mike has the backside A gap.

---

## OLB — two of them

**This is where the ball goes.** The front slants outward and the DEs turn everything back in, so runs end up in this player's lap. He has to make the tackle in a narrowing space.

**Stance** — Athletic stance, wrists on the knees. Same as Mike.

**Depth** — 4-5 yards. Same as Mike.

**First steps** — Two read steps at the snap. Same as Mike.

### Alignment — depends on what's in front of him

| Look | Where he lines up |
|---|---|
| **Default / nobody out there** | Stacked directly behind the DE |
| **There's a #2** | Apex: halfway between the DE and #2 |
| **Trips (he's got #3)** | Apex: halfway between the DE and #3 |
| **Nub side** | Apex: inside the DE, halfway to where the TE is lined up |

**He always has inside position.** That's built into how we line him up.

**Man coverage** — He has **#2** to his side.

- If the CB on his side flips away to the trips side, the OLB has **#1** instead. On a nub that's the TE.
- **Free blitz only when he has no coverage responsibility at all.**

**Run responsibility** — Be aggressive. **Step forward into the gap while it's narrow.** Do not sit and wait. An open-field tackle is much harder than one in a tight space.

### Trips coverage

Bring the **CB from the backside across to take #2.** Then:

| Receiver | Who has him |
|---|---|
| Strong #1 | Strong CB |
| Strong #2 | Backside CB, travels across |
| Strong #3 | Strong OLB, apexed between DE and #3 |
| Backside #1 (the TE on a nub) | Backside OLB — the CB flipped away, so he has #1 now |
| Back in the backfield | Mike |
| — | **S stays free in the middle** |

> The backside CB traveling pre-snap is the one piece of real communication in this defense. It is the hardest thing on the sheet for a 10-year-old.
>
> We will not see much nub TE. We're the unusual ones.

---

## CB — two of them

**Alignment** — **7 yards off. Outside leverage.**

**Man coverage** — **Take #1.** He moves, you move.

**Press** — Only when I call for it from the sideline.

**Run responsibility** — **Maintain outside contain. Force the runner inside of you.**

**The cue** — *"Stay with your man."*

> These are not our best tacklers. The DEs and OLBs are built to keep the ball away from them. The corner is the backstop, not the edge-setter.

---

## S (Safety) — last line of defense

**Depth** — 10-12 yards.

**Coverage** — **Almost always free.** No man.

**Headcount** — While the offense huddles, before every play: count the 10 players plus himself. If it's not 10, get the coach's attention to fix the substitution.

### Alignment by formation

| Look | Where he stands |
|---|---|
| **2x2** | Middle of the field |
| **2x1 or 3x1** | Apex between the end man on the line and the most inside receiver |

That shade puts him toward the side where the action is likely to be, while still keeping him mostly in the center of the field.

**The cue** — *"You're the last one. Be smart, read the play, arrive and clean up. Don't be risky and get burnt."*
